Professional teaching portfolio

Teaching & classroom practice

A progression from confident technology use to independent computational thinking and problem solving.

Explain and model

Break concepts into manageable steps, demonstrate the thinking behind a solution and use visual representations alongside technical language.

Check and adapt

Use questioning, whiteboards, short quizzes and practical checks to identify misconceptions and adjust the next task.

Practise independently

Move from guided examples to independent tasks, with scaffolds and extension questions appropriate to the learner.

Examples from my teaching

KS2: digital literacy and physical computing

Google Docs, Slides and Sheets support purposeful communication and data handling. Online safety, website creation and Sphero activities connect digital habits with sequencing and logical thinking.

KS3: programming, representation and the web

Scratch, App Lab and HTML introduce practical development. Binary, images and sound connect data representation with familiar digital media. AI literacy includes bias, fact-checking and responsible use.

GCSE: theory connected to practical examples

CPU/FDE, memory, storage, networks and security are supported by retrieval, worked calculations and exam-style questions. Python tracing, algorithms and programming develop the reasoning needed for Paper 2.

A Level: increasing depth and independence

CPU architecture, operating systems, algorithms, data structures, SQL and object-oriented programming develop technical depth. Practical examples include Python with Tkinter and SQLite, alongside LMC assembly activities.

Adaptive teaching and pastoral support

Modelling, scaffolded tasks, vocabulary support and regular checks for understanding help pupils with different starting points, including SEN and EAL learners. Form tutoring and previous boarding experience support my understanding of pupils beyond lessons.
