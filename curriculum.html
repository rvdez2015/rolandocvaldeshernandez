Curriculum Journey

2026–27 curriculum overview

This public overview is derived from my current curriculum planning and lesson tracker. It deliberately excludes student names, class-level attainment, dates and other pupil-identifiable or operational data.

The curriculum is designed as a coherent progression from digital literacy and safe technology use through computer systems, programming and computational thinking to OCR GCSE and A Level Computer Science.

KS2 • Foundations

Years 5–6

Digital literacy and safe, effective technology use form the foundation for later Computer Science.

  • Digital habits, online safety and responsible technology use
  • Google Docs, Slides, Sheets and presentation skills
  • Copyright, search literacy and recognising unreliable content
  • Web-page creation and digital media
  • Scratch programming and computational thinking
  • Robotics / physical computing with Sphero
KS3 • Core CS

Years 7–9

Students move from digital literacy into increasingly formal Computer Science concepts and practical development.

  • Computer systems: motherboard, CPU, memory, storage and I/O
  • Networks and protocols
  • Cybersecurity, malware, phishing and safe practice
  • HTML and web development
  • App design and App Lab
  • Binary, images, sound and data representation
  • Programming development and problem solving
AI Literacy

Responsible AI education

AI is introduced as both a computing concept and a digital-literacy responsibility.

  • What AI is and where it is used
  • AI bias and the reliability of generated outputs
  • Prompting and AI-generated media
  • Ethical and responsible use
  • Critical evaluation rather than passive acceptance of AI output
KS4 • OCR J277

Years 10–11

Specification-led GCSE study builds directly on KS3 systems, programming and data-representation knowledge.

  • Systems architecture, CPU registers and the FDE cycle
  • CPU performance and embedded systems
  • Primary/secondary storage and data units
  • Number systems, binary arithmetic and shifts
  • Character, image and sound representation
  • Networks, security, algorithms and programming
  • Retrieval, assessment and examination preparation
KS5 • OCR H446/H046

A Level progression

Existing KS5 curriculum and teaching evidence extends the pathway into advanced Computer Science: CPU architecture and performance, algorithms, programming, data structures, databases and SQL, object-oriented programming, computational thinking and the programming project/NEA.

Curriculum leadership and assessment

Curriculum planning combines clear sequencing, retrieval, explicit modelling, adaptive teaching, practical application and assessment for learning. Formative and summative evidence is used to identify misconceptions and inform subsequent teaching, while examination analysis feeds back into curriculum refinement and intervention planning.
